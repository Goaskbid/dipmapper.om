Unique water experiences

80 places where water does something you will remember: surf waves in the middle of cities, pools floating on rivers and harbours, rooftop swims, sauna boats and hot springs. Every entry was researched from the operator, the city or an encyclopedia and carries its sources; statuses were checked on 10 October 2026.

Surf waves in the city and on rivers

Eisbach Wave (Eisbachwelle)

Munich · DE

The Eisbach is a two-kilometre side arm of the Isar that runs through the Englischer Garten, and its famous wave forms right after the bridge beside the Haus der Kunst on Prinzregentenstrasse. Surfers have ridden it since the early 1970s and it was officially permitted in 2010. The wave is about a metre high over water that can be only 40 cm deep, so it belongs to skilled surfers and playboaters, while everyone else watches from the bridge railing.

Good to know: Experts only. Reopened for surfing on 8 May 2026 under a city order: a self-releasing leash is mandatory, no surfing from 22:00, and the posted rules apply. Swimming is prohibited; a surfer died here in April 2025.

Flosslaende River Wave (Floßlände)

Munich · DE

This is where Munich surfers learn before they try the Eisbach. The wave forms in a raft canal that branches off the Isar beside the Thalkirchen campsite on Zentralländstrasse, next to a small footbridge. It is lower and more forgiving than the city-centre wave, but flow depends on the river and on hydropower operation, so it is mainly a summer spot. Rocky walls on both sides and a shallow, stony bed teach you to fall flat.

Good to know: Shallow water with rock walls; for surfers, not a swimming spot.

Leinewelle (Leine River Wave)

Hannover · DE

Built by the Leinewelle association on the Altstadtufer, the western bank of the Leine near Schlossstrasse, this standing wave sits right in the city centre rather than on a remote river stretch. It went into test operation in January 2023 and is now used year-round by surfers, school classes and youth groups. In 2026 the German rapid surfing championships were held here during the Finals, and a temporary hub with changing rooms, toilets and seating was set up on the Schlossstrasse car park.

Good to know: Open year-round according to the City of Hannover (June 2026), used by surfers, school classes and youth groups. A temporary 'Leinewelle hub' with changing rooms and toilets is being set up on the Schlossstraße car park for about three years. Confirm session rules with the operator (Leinewelle e.V.).

Bremgarten Reuss Wave

Bremgarten AG · CH

Bremgarten, a small walled town on the Reuss in the canton of Aargau about 23 km from Zurich, is often described as the heart of Swiss river surfing. When the river runs high in early summer, a natural wave forms near the old town and draws surfers from across the Zurich region, with spectators lining the bank. Surfertoday reports that surfers have ridden the spot for decades, calls it relatively beginner-friendly and puts the river surf season at roughly May to August.

Good to know: Natural river: cold water and strong current; only go with river-surf experience or a local guide.

Thun Muehleplatz Aare Wave (Mühleplatz)

Thun · CH

In Thun's old town the Aare flows out of Lake Thun through regulated sluices beside the Mühleplatz, a square of cafés and bars right on the water. When the gates are set to the right flow, a surfable wave forms in the middle of town, and Swissinfo has reported on surfers riding it in cold water in front of curious passers-by. It is a spot for experienced river surfers; for everyone else the bridges and terraces make good viewing points.

Good to know: Unverified details: wave timing and any local rules were not confirmed from an official source. Strong, cold current at the weir.

Urbansurf Zurich

Zurich · CH

Next to Frau Gerolds Garten and a short walk from Hardbrücke station, Urbansurf runs an endless-wave platform at Geroldstrasse 11c in the middle of Zurich's former industrial district. Sessions are booked in 30-minute slots with up to six surfers at a time, and professional instructors are present, so it works for complete novices as well as experienced surfers. Padded walls and sufficient water depth are built in for safety, and watching from the platform costs nothing.

Alaia Bay (Alaïa Bay)

Sion · CH

Alaïa Bay in Sion opened on 1 May 2021 as Switzerland's first wave pool, using Wavegarden technology to produce repeatable waves for beginners through to advanced surfers. The setting is what sets it apart: the pool lies on the floor of the Rhône valley in Valais, surrounded by high mountains, so you can surf in the morning and be in the Alps by afternoon. It was Wavegarden's fifth facility worldwide.

The Wave Bristol

Bristol · GB

On farmland at Easter Compton on the northern edge of Bristol, The Wave opened in 2019 with a Wavegarden-built lake 300 m long, 100 m wide and 1.5 m deep. A central pier splits it into left and right breaks, and settings run from half-metre whitewater for first-timers to two-metre barrels. Up to 80 surfers can use it at once, and the site adds a clubhouse, restaurant, camping and woodland trails. Solar panels installed in 2023 help power the waves.

URBNSURF Melbourne

It lets you swim in the river setting without swimming in the river itself, and a bar with DJs sits by the entrance.

Islands Brygge Harbour Bath (Havnebadet Islands Brygge)

Copenhagen · DK

The permanent bath from 2003 has five pools, including two 50-metre lanes, two children's pools and a diving pool with three- and five-metre boards over water seven metres deep, with space for around 600 people. On summer evenings the quay lawns fill with locals coming straight from work.

Paris · FR

Opened in 2006, the Joséphine Baker pool floats on a barge on the Seine below the Bibliothèque François-Mitterrand in the 13th arrondissement and is the only floating pool in Paris. Frauenbad Stadthausquai (Frauenbadi)

By day it is for women only; on certain summer evenings it turns into the Barfussbar, open to everyone.

Good to know: Free swimming in the Limmat at this point is prohibited.

River swimming in the city

Rhine Swimming in Basel (Rheinschwimmen)

Basel · CH

Through the summer, people in Basel walk upstream along the Kleinbasel bank, pack their clothes into a fish-shaped waterproof bag called a Wickelfisch and let the current carry them past the old town and under the bridges. It is a daily after-work ritual rather than a tourist activity, and once a year the city holds an official organised Rhine swim. The Rhine is a busy shipping route, so the cantonal police publish rules for safe swimming.

Good to know: Strong current and commercial shipping; stay near the bank, keep clear of bridge piers and moored ships, and follow Basel police rules.

Aare Swimming at Marzili (Aareschwimmen)

Bern · CH

The Marzili lies directly below the Federal Palace and is free to enter. The local routine is simple: walk upstream along the riverbank path to the Eichholz area, get into the green Aare and drift back down, then grab a rail and get out at the Marzili. Besides the river, there is a 50-metre pool, a diving pool, a children's pool, lawns and the women-only Paradiesli. On peak days more than 10,000 people come.

Good to know: Aare swimming is recommended only for experienced swimmers: cold, fast water.

Thermal waters

Blue Lagoon

Grindavík · IS

Iceland's best-known lagoon sits in a black lava field on the Reykjanes peninsula near Grindavík, not far from Keflavík airport. Its milky blue colour comes from silica in geothermal seawater, and bathers smear the white mud on their faces while steam drifts across the water. It is the obvious stop on the way to or from a flight, and the contrast of pale water and dark lava is unlike any other bath.

Good to know: The Reykjanes peninsula has had repeated eruptions since 2023 and the lagoon has closed at times; check status before travelling.

Waitomo Glowworm Caves

Waitomo · NZ

In the limestone country of New Zealand's North Island, the Waitomo caves are home to glowworms, the larvae of a fungus gnat that hang glowing threads from the cave roof to catch insects. Tours walk through the cave chambers and then glide by boat in silence through the grotto, where the ceiling looks like a starry sky. Tours run all year.

Good to know: No photography in the glowworm grotto.

Where

Season

Getting there
Stay silent on the boat section; noise and light make the glowworms dim.

Tomales Bay Bioluminescence Kayaking

Point Reyes (California) · US

About 40 miles north of San Francisco, Tomales Bay lies inside Point Reyes National Seashore, a long, sheltered inlet along the San Andreas fault. In late summer and autumn, dinoflagellates bloom and the water sparks blue around paddles and fish. Several companies run guided night kayak tours, which makes it the most accessible bioluminescence trip from the Bay Area.

Good to know: Cold water: dress for immersion.
