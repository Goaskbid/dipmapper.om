Florida

Who needs what

  • Non-residents aged 16 and over need a Florida licence for fresh water and for salt water – catch and release included. Out-of-state licences are not valid.
  • Under 16: no licence needed.
  • No licence needed on a licensed for-hire charter, on a boat whose operator holds a recreational saltwater vessel licence, from a licensed pier, or on free fishing days.

Prices

Saltwater – non-resident 3 days$17
Saltwater – non-resident 7 days$30
Saltwater – non-resident year$47

Where to get it

  • Online from the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC), by phone or at licence agents.

Rules to know

  • Tarpon need a separate tag.
  • Being on the National Saltwater Angler Registry does not replace the Florida licence.

Source: FWC – Visitors' licences · FWC – Saltwater licences and permits

California

Who needs what

  • Non-residents aged 16 and over need a sport fishing licence; one- and two-day licences are for everyone, the ten-day licence only for non-residents.
  • No licence is needed from a public pier in ocean waters (report cards still apply) and on Free Fishing Days.

Prices

1 day (2026 / 2027)$21.09 / $21.86
2 days$32.40 / $33.48
10 days – non-resident$64.54 / $66.96
Year – non-resident$174.14 / $180.36
Ocean Enhancement Validation (south of Point Arguello)$7.30 / $7.56 – not needed with a 1- or 2-day licence
Spiny lobster report card$12.70 / $13.22

Where to get it

  • Online from the California Department of Fish and Wildlife or at licence agents.

Rules to know

  • The abalone fishery is closed.
  • Sturgeon: catch and release only, with a report card.
  • A Second Rod Validation lets you fish two rods in inland waters.

Source: CDFW – Sport fishing licences and report cards

Hawaii

Who needs what

  • Non-residents aged 15 and over need the non-resident marine recreational fishing licence to fish in the ocean. Active-duty military and their minor children are exempt.

Prices

1 day$20
7 days$40
Year$70

Where to get it

  • Online at fishing.hawaii.gov (processing fees may apply).

Source: Hawaii DLNR – Nonresident recreational marine fishing licence

Alaska

Who needs what

  • Non-residents aged 16 and over need a sport fishing licence for fresh and salt water; under 16 need neither licence nor king salmon stamp.
  • Fishing for king salmon also needs a king salmon stamp.

Prices

Licence – 1 / 3 / 7 / 14 days$15 / $30 / $45 / $75
Licence – year$100
King salmon stamp – 1 / 3 / 7 / 14 days / year$15 / $30 / $45 / $75 / $100

Rules to know

  • Non-resident anglers under 16 need a harvest record card.

Source: Alaska Department of Fish and Game – 2026 licensing (Southwest regulations)

Largemouth bass Micropterus salmoides

Freshwater Florida, Texas and southern lakes.

How to recognise it

  • Jaw reaches past the eye
  • Dark broken band along the side
  • Deep notch between the two dorsal fins

Time of day: Dawn and dusk near weeds and wood.

Bait and method

Smallmouth bass Micropterus dolomieu

Freshwater Great Lakes and northern rivers.

How to recognise it

  • Bronze-brown with faint vertical bars
  • Jaw ends below the eye
  • Red eye

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Morning and evening over rocks.

Bait and method

  • Spinning: tubes, small crankbaits and jigs over rocky points
  • Fly: crayfish and streamer patterns

Walleye Sander vitreus

Freshwater Midwest lakes.

How to recognise it

  • Big glassy eyes – it sees well in the dark
  • White tip on the lower tail lobe
  • Close relative of the European zander

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Dusk, night and dark, windy days.

Bait and method

  • Jigging: jig and minnow or soft plastic close to the bottom
  • Trolling: crawler harnesses and crankbaits along drop-offs

Muskellunge Muskie · Esox masquinongy

Freshwater Wisconsin and Minnesota.

How to recognise it

  • Like a huge pike but with dark bars or spots on a light body
  • Pointed tail lobes
  • The "fish of 10,000 casts"

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Low light and before weather fronts.

Bait and method

  • Big lures: bucktails, jerkbaits and swimbaits with a figure-eight at the boat

Chinook (king) salmon King salmon · Oncorhynchus tshawytscha

Sea and rivers Alaska and the Pacific Northwest.

How to recognise it

  • The largest Pacific salmon
  • Black gums at the base of the teeth
  • Spots on the back and on both lobes of the tail

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Early morning on the tide and in the river.

Bait and method

  • Trolling: herring or plugs behind a flasher in salt water
  • Drift fishing: roe or yarn in river runs

Steelhead / rainbow trout Oncorhynchus mykiss

Sea and rivers Pacific Northwest and the Great Lakes.

How to recognise it

  • Pink to red stripe along the flank
  • Small black spots on back, fins and tail
  • Sea-run fish are much more silver

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Low light in winter rivers.

Bait and method

  • Drift fishing: roe bags or beads under a float
  • Fly: swung flies in cold water

Striped bass Morone saxatilis

Sea and rivers East Coast from Maine to the Carolinas.

How to recognise it

  • Seven or eight dark stripes along a silver body
  • Two separate dorsal fins
  • Big mouth

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Night, dawn and moving tides.

Bait and method

  • Spinning: swimbaits and plugs along surf and rocks
  • Bait: clams, bunker or eels on the bottom

Red drum (redfish) Sciaenops ocellatus

Sea Gulf coast and Carolinas.

How to recognise it

  • Copper-bronze body
  • One or more black spots near the tail
  • Underslung mouth for feeding on the bottom

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Moving tides on flats and marsh edges.

Bait and method

  • Sight fishing: gold spoons, shrimp or crab on the flats
  • Surf: cut mullet on the bottom in autumn

Snook Centropomus undecimalis

Sea and rivers South Florida.

How to recognise it

  • Bold black lateral line
  • Sloping forehead and protruding lower jaw
  • Yellow pelvic fins

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Night around lights and bridges, moving tide.

Bait and method

  • Spinning: jigs and swimbaits on outgoing tides
  • Live bait: pilchards and shrimp

Tarpon Megalops atlanticus

Sea and rivers Florida Keys and Gulf.

How to recognise it

  • Huge silver scales
  • Upturned mouth
  • Spectacular jumps

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Dawn and dusk; nights in passes.

Bait and method

  • Fly: large streamers to rolling fish
  • Live bait: crabs and mullet in the passes

Bonefish Albula vulpes

Sea Florida Keys.

How to recognise it

  • Silver, almost transparent "grey ghost" of the flats
  • Pointed snout, underslung mouth
  • Very fast first run

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Rising and falling tides on shallow flats.

Bait and method

  • Fly: small shrimp patterns cast to tailing fish – almost always released

Yellowfin tuna Thunnus albacares

Sea Gulf and offshore Atlantic.

How to recognise it

  • Dark blue back with a yellow side stripe
  • Long yellow second dorsal and anal fins
  • Bright yellow finlets

When

JFMAMJJASOND

Time of day: Dawn feeding frenzies with birds.

Bait and method

  • Trolling: skirted lures and cedar plugs

Quick answers

Do I need a fishing licence in the USA?

Fresh water: State licence. Sea: State licence. Non-residents aged 16 and over need a Florida licence for fresh water and for salt water – catch and release included. Out-of-state licences are not valid.

How much does a fishing licence cost in the USA?

Saltwater – non-resident 3 days: $17; Saltwater – non-resident 7 days: $30; Saltwater – non-resident year: $47; 1 day (2026 / 2027): $21.09 / $21.86.

Where do I buy a fishing licence in the USA?

Online from the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC), by phone or at licence agents.
