    Lakes and their rules

    Free angling from the shore is a Swiss speciality: on many big lakes anyone may fish from the bank with one simple rod – no licence, no course. Everything else (boats, lures with treble hooks, more rods) needs a cantonal patent, and annual patents need the SaNa certificate.

    Lake Zurich (canton Zurich shore)

    Shore fishing: Free angling from the shore for everyone, without patent or SaNa: one rod or line with a single bait and a single barbless hook. Natural baits and food are allowed, artificial flies only on a fly rod (hook size 8 at most); no baitfish. The same free right applies on Greifensee, Pfäffikersee and Türlersee.

    Good to know: Boat fishing and more gear need a cantonal patent (webshop or eFJ app from mid-December; annual patents need the SaNa).

    Lake Zurich/Obersee, Walensee and Lake Constance (canton St. Gallen shores)

    Shore fishing: Free angling from the shore without a permit: one rod or a single line with one bait; natural baits and food, no live baitfish. On Lake Constance also from shore walls; on Walensee artificial flies are allowed; on Zürichsee/Obersee flies up to hook size 8.

    Good to know: Local rules on fishing times, species and catch sizes apply; animal-welfare law applies even without SaNa. Wardens check.

    Official source: Canton St. Gallen – Freiangelrecht

    Lake Constance – Obersee (Swiss shore permit)

    Shore fishing: Thurgau shore: a shore-fishing permit is needed (CHF 60 a year or CHF 30 a month, plus the fishing levy). Residents of Thurgau and St. Gallen buy it in their home canton. Carry the permit, proof of competence and ID.

    Good to know: Closed seasons start and end at 12:00 noon. Fishing is banned 1 Nov – 31 Jan in four protected zones (Goldach, Steinach, Luxburger Bucht, Güttingen).

    Lake Lucerne

    Shore fishing: No free angling right is listed in the source; fish with a patent from one of the lake cantons. Patents for more than one month need proof of competence (e.g. the Swiss Sportfischerbrevet / SaNa).

    Good to know: Live bait is banned; dead baitfish must come from Lake Lucerne. Many methods allow at most two rods. Nase and all crayfish are protected all year. Rules text as amended in 2009 – check the current version with the canton.

    Fish guide

    Twelve fish you are most likely to meet in Swiss lakes and rivers – how to recognise them, when they bite and what they take. The month strip shows the season (light) and the best months (dark); a closed season on your lake always wins.

    Perch Egli / Flussbarsch · Perca fluviatilis

    How to recognise it

    • Dark vertical bands on the flanks
    • Two separate dorsal fins, the first spiny with a black spot at its rear
    • Strong spine on the gill cover
    • Reddish lower fins

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Afternoon and evening best, mornings good.

    Bait and method

    • Float (Pose): red worms, a piece of lobworm or a bundle of maggots at mid-water – maggots work well in winter
    • Ledger (Grund): worm on a simple bottom rig
    • Spinning: small soft lures 5–8 cm on 2–5 g jig heads, twisters, small spinners; stop-and-go retrieve – needs a permit, not free angling
    • Winter: schools sit deep, often 16–20 m; work crayfish imitations slowly

    Pike Hecht · Esox lucius

    How to recognise it

    • Long body with the dorsal fin set far back near the tail
    • Long, flat "duck-bill" snout full of strong teeth
    • Green with pale, bean-shaped spots

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Morning and evening best, afternoons good.

    Bait and method

    • Spinning: wobblers, spinners and spinnerbaits, soft jig lures
    • Float or ledger: dead baitfish – in winter on or near the bottom; live baitfish are banned on Lake Lucerne and in free angling
    • Free angling: a single barbless hook and no baitfish – real pike fishing needs a cantonal permit

    Fact file

    Zander (pikeperch) Zander · Sander lucioperca

    How to recognise it

    • Pike-like slim body, but two separate dorsal fins like a perch
    • Visible fang teeth, no spine on the gill cover
    • Glassy eyes, faint dark saddles

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Evening and night best, mornings good.

    Bait and method

    • Jig / drop shot: soft lures 10–15 cm in natural colours near the bottom; glow heads at dusk and night
    • Ledger: dead baitfish, best in autumn and winter

    Fact file

    Whitefish Felchen / Balchen · Coregonus

    How to recognise it

    • Silvery, slim body with large scales
    • Small adipose fin between dorsal fin and tail
    • Small mouth that does not reach the eye, pointed snout

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Daytime; follow the school depth.

    Bait and method

    • Hegene: a long leader with 3–5 small nymphs fished vertically from a boat: spring 10–25 m, summer 30–60 m, autumn 20–40 m, winter 40–70 m; lift metre by metre to find the school
    • Rules: closed all year on Lake Constance at the moment, closed in late autumn elsewhere

    Fact file

    Lake trout Seeforelle · Salmo trutta lacustris

    How to recognise it

    • Silvery with sparse black, star-shaped spots
    • No red spots
    • Adipose fin

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Morning and evening best.

    Bait and method

    • Trolling / spinning: spoons, spinners and small wobblers – mostly from a boat with a permit
    • Rules: big minimum sizes (40–50 cm) and a long winter closed season

    Fact file

    Brown trout Bachforelle · Salmo trutta

    How to recognise it

    • Round red spots with pale rims, plus black spots
    • Adipose fin, often with an orange edge
    • Golden-brown flanks

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Afternoon and evening best.

    Bait and method

    • Spinning: small spinners and wobblers 4–7 cm; in streams cast across the current and let the lure swing
    • Float or ledger: worms, bee maggots, mealworms
    • Fly: dry flies and nymphs – where fly fishing is allowed

    How to recognise it

    • Black spots also on the tail fin

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Bait and method

    • Spinning: spinners and small wobblers
    • Float: dough baits and worms

    Arctic char Seesaibling / Rötel · Salvelinus umbla

    How to recognise it

    • Blue-grey or greenish flanks with small pale, red or orange spots
    • Orange-red belly in spawning dress
    • White leading edges on the lower fins

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Lake char: mornings best.

    Bait and method

    • Ledger or float in deep water: bee maggots and small natural baits
    • Rules: closed season from October or November to December

    Fact file

    Grayling Äsche · Thymallus thymallus

    How to recognise it

    • Huge, sail-like dorsal fin with more than 16 soft rays
    • Small mouth and adipose fin
    • Silvery-grey with a few dark spots

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Afternoon and evening.

    Bait and method

    • Rules first: protected all year in Lake Zurich and Lake Thun; in rivers only with a cantonal permit and in season

    Fact file

    Carp Karpfen · Cyprinus carpio

    How to recognise it

    • Deep body with one long dorsal fin
    • Four barbels: two long, two short
    • Deeply forked tail

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Morning and evening best, nights good.

    Bait and method

    • Ledger (Grund): boilies, sweetcorn, tiger nuts, paste baits, bread – with a little groundbait
    • Float: sweetcorn or bread close to the margins

    Fact file

    Wels catfish Wels · Silurus glanis

    How to recognise it

    • No scales, slimy skin
    • Six barbels: two very long on the upper jaw
    • Tiny dorsal fin and a very long anal fin

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Evening and night best.

    Bait and method

    • Ledger (Grund): dead baitfish, fish pieces or a bunch of lobworms – strong tackle
    • Where: native only to a few Swiss waters north of the Alps

    Fact file

    Burbot Trüsche · Lota lota

    How to recognise it

    • The only freshwater cod: one barbel under the chin
    • Long second dorsal and anal fins
    • Marbled brown-olive skin

    When

    JFMAMJJASOND

    Time of day: Evening and night.

    Bait and method

    • Ledger (Grund): worms, small fish or fish pieces on the bottom in deep, cool, stony water – the classic winter night fish

    Float, ledger, spinning, fly or Hegene?

    Float (Pose)

    A float holds the bait at a set depth and shows the bite. Ideal for perch, roach and trout from the shore, and the classic free-angling rig.

    Ledger (Grundangel)

    A weight keeps the bait on the bottom. For carp, eel, catfish and burbot – fish that feed on the bottom, often at dusk and at night.

    Spinning

    Casting and retrieving a lure – spinner, spoon, wobbler or soft lure. For pike, zander, perch and trout. Treble hooks and lures usually need a patent; free angling allows a single barbless hook only.

    Fly

    An artificial fly cast with a fly rod and line. For trout and grayling; on Lake Zurich free anglers may use flies only on a fly rod, hook size 8 at most.

    Hegene

    The Swiss whitefish rig: 3–5 tiny nymphs on a long leader, fished vertically from a boat and lifted metre by metre until the school is found.

    Famous fishing spots worldwide

    23 places anglers travel for – with the fish, the best months, the method, the permit you need and how to get there. Sorted from where you are once you share your location.

    River Spey Speyside, Scotland

    JFMAMJJASOND
    Fish
    Atlantic salmon, sea trout
    Method
    Fly fishing (Spey casting was born here)
    What you need
    No national licence in Scotland – you buy a day or week permit for one stretch ("beat") of the river.
    Getting there
    Grantown-on-Spey, Aberlour and Fochabers; association water at Grantown is the affordable start.
    Good to know
    The season runs from February to the end of September. Most salmon are released; follow the beat's catch-and-release rules and use single barbless hooks where asked.

    England and Wales

    Rod licence

    Who needs what
    Needed for salmon, trout, freshwater fish, smelt and eel – also on private lakes and club waters. Under 13: none needed. 13–16: free junior licence.
    Where to get it
    Online from the UK government; you also need the landowner's or club's permission.
    Good to know
    Not showing a licence when asked can cost up to £2,500. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own rules.
    • GOV.UK – When you need a rod licence

    Ireland

    Salmon and sea trout licence

    Who needs what
    Needed only when fishing for salmon or sea trout; licences run from one day to one year.
    Where to get it
    Online through Inland Fisheries Ireland's angling website.
    Good to know
    Comes with the wild salmon and sea trout tagging rules: bag limits, catch-and-release rivers, gill tags and a logbook to return.
    • gov.ie – Buy a salmon fishing licence

    France

    Carte de pêche (freshwater)

    Who needs what
    Needed in open fresh waters: it proves you joined a local angling association (AAPPMA) and paid the water fee. Not needed in enclosed waters, nor on the national fishing day (first Sunday of June).
    Where to get it
    Online at cartedepeche.fr, from the local AAPPMA or shops showing the "cartes de pêche" sign; each association sets its price.
    Good to know
    Trout waters (1st category) open from the second Saturday of March to the third Sunday of September; other waters mostly all year, with closed seasons for pike and trout.
    • Service-Public.fr – Recreational freshwater angling

    Germany – Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

    Tourist fishing licence + permit

    Who needs what
    Holidaymakers always need two documents: the tourist fishing licence (from age 14; 28 days, €23 the first time, can be extended once in the same year) and a permit for the water you fish.
    Where to get it
    Licence at 160+ offices or online at erlaubnis.angeln-mv.de; permits from LALLF, hejfish, Fiskado, fishing lodges or tourist offices – also for the Baltic coast.
    Good to know
    Every German state has its own rules; this tourist licence is the Baltic-coast state's scheme. Closed seasons and minimum sizes come in the brochure with the licence.
    • Visit Mecklenburg-Vorpommern – Fishing licences

    Norway

    Sea: minimum sizes and export quota · Fresh water: fee + local permit

    Who needs what
    At sea everyone must keep to the minimum sizes, and there is a limit on how much fish you may take out of Norway. In rivers and lakes you need a permit from whoever holds the fishing rights; for salmon, sea trout and Arctic char a national fishing fee is added (not for under-18s).
    Where to get it
    Local permits from tourist offices or sports shops; fishing camps report your catch to the authorities.
    Good to know
    A boating licence is needed for boats of 8–15 m or engines over 25 hp if you were born in 1980 or later.
    • Norwegian Directorate of Fisheries – Sea angling rules
    • Norwegian Environment Agency – Rivers and lakes

    Austria

    Guest fishing card + water permit

    Who needs what
    Visitors need the guest fishing card of the federal state (usually valid up to four weeks, roughly €5–25, shown with photo ID) plus a day, week, month or night permit for the exact water.
    Where to get it
    Through the regional tourist offices and the "Fishing waters Austria" listings – each state and each water sets its own price.
    Good to know
    Every federal state has its own fishing law, closed seasons and minimum sizes. Classic waters: Weissensee, Attersee, Lake Neusiedl.
    • Austrian National Tourist Office – Fishing in Austria

    Italy

    Sea: free registration · Fresh water: regional licence

    Who needs what
    Sea angling needs no licence, but every recreational sea angler must first register the activity with the Ministry of Agriculture (valid three years). In fresh water each region issues its own licence, and many waters need an extra local permit.
    Where to get it
    Sea registration online with the ministry, through angling associations or at the harbour office; freshwater licences from the region and permits from local clubs or shops.
    Good to know
    If you cannot show your registration at a check you must stop fishing and register within 10 days. Marine protected areas have their own rules.
    • Decree of 6 December 2010 on recreational sea fishing (FAOLEX)

    Spain

    Licence from the coastal region

    Who needs what
    Recreational sea fishing – from the shore, from a boat or underwater – needs a licence from the coastal autonomous community (for example the Balearics, Catalonia, Andalusia or the Canaries). Fresh waters are regulated by each region too.
    Where to get it
    From the autonomous community, mostly online; boats fishing protected species need an extra permit from the state.
    Good to know
    Only listed species with minimum sizes may be kept. Bluefin tuna: catch and release only, closed from mid-October to mid-June. Swordfish may not be caught. Marine reserves only with special permission.
    • Spanish Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food – Recreational sea fishing FAQ

    Sweden

    Free on the coast and the five big lakes

    Who needs what
    With hand-held tackle (rod, handline) visitors fish licence-free along the coast and in Vänern, Vättern, Mälaren, Hjälmaren and Storsjön. Every other lake and river needs a fishing permit (fiskekort).
    Where to get it
    Fiskekort from the local fishing association, campsite, shop or online sellers such as iFiske.
    Good to know
    Nets, trolling and tip-ups need a licence even in free waters. Keep 100 m away from fixed fishing gear and fish farms; take every line and hook home.
    • Swedish Environmental Protection Agency – Hunting and fishing

    Croatia

    Sea licence – even from the shore

    Who needs what
    Recreational sea fishing needs a licence whether you fish from the shore or a boat: one day, several days or a year. Fresh water has its own separate permits.
    Where to get it
    Daily and multi-day licences online (confirmation by email), at fisheries offices or authorised sellers; annual licences only 1 December – 1 March.
    Good to know
    Up to two handlines, trolling lines or squid jigs; from a boat a rod of up to 30 lb. National parks, nature parks and special reserves need special licences.
    • gov.hr – Marine recreational and sport fisheries

    Fly fishing

    Where fly anglers go: Austria's rivers (guest card plus water permit, see above), and the salmon rivers of Scotland, Ireland and Norway – where you can also watch salmon leap in late summer.

    • Großarler AcheSalzburg · Alpine stream

    The basics

    • Licence: cantons issue fishing patents. Annual patents need the SaNa certificate (a short course on humane handling of fish); free angling rights and short-term patents do not.
    • Measure and release: fish below the minimum size, in their closed season or protected must go back at once, handled with wet hands. Size is measured from the snout to the end of the tail fin.
    • Keep: in canton Bern, legal fish caught under free angling must be kept, stunned with a firm blow to the head and killed at once – never kept alive.
    • Banned: live baitfish under free angling and on Lake Lucerne; barbed hooks under free angling in Zurich and Bern.
    • Protected: grayling all year on Lake Zurich and Lake Thun; whitefish all year on Lake Constance (Obersee) at present; nase and crayfish all year on Lake Lucerne.
    • Spots: OpenStreetMap shows places volunteers mapped – not permission. Private land, harbours, bathing areas and nature reserves have their own rules.
