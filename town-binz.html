Ostseebad Binz

Rügen's grand resort: white villas, a long pier and chalk cliffs.

Binz is Rügen's most elegant seaside resort: a 5 km sandy beach, white spa-architecture villas, a 370 m pier and a promenade full of cafés. The steam train Rasender Roland and the chalk cliffs of Jasmund National Park are close.

Insider: what you actually want to know

Do this

Walk the 370 m pier, take the steam train Rasender Roland, see the white chalk cliffs of Jasmund and eat a Fischbrötchen on the promenade.

What actually goes wrong

The only real risk is the Baltic cold and Kurtaxe controls at the beach entrances. Very safe. Watch out for bike lanes (cyclists will not stop) and strong currents near groynes on the Baltic and North Sea.

Tipping

Round up or 5–10%; say the total when paying instead of leaving cash on the table.

Good to know

Many places are still cash-only. Shops close on Sunday. Nude beaches (FKK) are common and normal.

Emergency numbers in Germany

112Fire and ambulance; general emergency

Save these before you go. When you call, say first which town and beach you are at.

Kurtaxe: the beach costs a little

Almost every Baltic and North Sea resort charges a visitor tax (Kurtaxe, usually €2–4 per adult per day in season). Your hotel adds it and gives you a Kurkarte – on Sylt, Norderney and many Baltic beaches you need it to get onto the beach.

Source: UK travel advice
Strandkorb, FKK and dog beaches

Rent the hooded wicker beach chair (Strandkorb) by the day or week – book ahead in July and August. Nude (FKK) sections are normal and signposted; dogs only on marked dog beaches in summer.

Source: UK travel advice
Sundays and cash

Shops close on Sundays outside resort areas, and many beach kiosks and small restaurants still prefer cash.

Source: UK travel advice
